# Book of knowledge

> **What this is:** every idea, document and item of the specifier, written like a glossary. Each row says what the item is and where it sits in the flow. It also says what the item takes in, what it gives out, and the gate where you decide.
>
> **What it is not:** how to run the kit, which is `README.md`. It is not the rules a skill follows either. Those are in `.claude/skills/_shared/`. It never overrides a skill: where they differ, the skill is right, and this book is rebuilt.
>
> **Who uses it:** a specifier, the BA or the PO. Use it to look up what something is, and where it fits.

**Being rebuilt:** handling a change. The change workflow is being redesigned, so part 9 lists its pieces as they are today, and says no more. Everything else in this book is the kit as it stands.

### People, skills and gates

| Term | What it means | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| **the specifiers** | You: the BA and the PO. You are the only people who decide. Skills read, draft, check and record. Programs check, route and generate. Neither decides anything. Anyone else, such as the client or an architect, sends what they say as a file in the sources folder. | • every gate<br>• conventions.md, section 1 |
| **skill, workflow and program** | A skill writes one document and holds its gate. A workflow runs a few skills in order, for one piece, then stops. A program is code in the tools folder. It checks, routes or builds a file, and has no gate. Nothing runs everything at once. | • README.md, section 2<br>• README.md, section 3<br>• the tools folder |
| **running on its own** | You can call any skill without its workflow. It behaves the same. It finds its inputs from the project name, runs `scaffold-project.cjs` first, and stops and names the input when one is missing or not accepted. | • every skill<br>• README.md, section 5 |
| **a mode** | The first thing a skill says when it starts: what kind of run this is. The modes are a new build, picking up where it stopped, a change, and nothing to do. `glossary-author` also has a re-run, and a mode for adding words from a request. | • `things-author`<br>• `data-model-author`<br>• `data-model-workflow`<br>• `glossary-author` |
| **a gate** | A decision review gate, or gate for short. It is the point where a skill shows you its whole draft, asks its questions one at a time, then asks whether to accept the document. Nothing goes on until you answer. | • every skill with a judgement<br>• decision-review-gates.md |
| **one gate per skill** | Each skill drafts the whole document, runs its check, then holds one gate. The one exception is `glossary-author`, with Gate 1 for the words and Gate 2 for the definitions. | • every skill<br>• conventions.md, section 6 |
| **the three blocks** | What the check program prints for the gate. You read these first, so the facts come from a program, not from the AI. "For the person" holds the facts for the first message. "One line" is the check result in one line, for each later decision. "For the file" is the check record, pasted into the document. | • every gate<br>• every check program<br>• `grade-questions.cjs` |
| **the order at a gate** | Every gate goes in the same order. First the "For the person" blocks. Then the Must answer questions, one at a time. Then the Should answer questions, one at a time. Then the Can move ahead questions, as one list. Last, the gate's own question. | every gate |
| **the gate's own question** | The last question at a gate, about the document itself, such as "Accept the FRD?". It is the only yes or no question. Its answers are Accept, or Change something first. | every gate |
| **the shape of a decision** | Every question at a gate has the same parts: why I'm asking, an example, what it changes with its impact and severity, the real answers with why you'd pick each and what happens next, "I don't know yet", the recommendation, and the next best action. The answers are real choices, never yes or no. | • every gate<br>• decision-review-gates.md, rule 4 |
| **names, not codes** | At a gate, the skill names things in words: a feature by its name, a requirement by its title, a rule by its words. A code, such as F-03, shows only in brackets after the name. You never have to read a code to decide. | • every gate<br>• decision-review-gates.md, rule 2 |
| **"I don't know yet"** | An answer offered with every question. The recommended answer is used, marked Proposed, and the question stays open in the Question log. | • every gate<br>• decision record<br>• status.cjs |
| **going back to a decision** | At a gate, you can say "go back to Decision" and its number. The skill asks it again. Your new answer is a new entry that starts "Replaces Decision n:". The earlier entry stays. Skipping a question counts as "I don't know yet". | every gate |
| **the third "no"** | After your third "no" at the same gate in one run, the skill stops redrafting. It offers: park it, accept it as it is with your remaining points as questions, or one more round. | • every gate<br>• `park.cjs` |
| **parked** | A piece set aside, at the back of the queue, so other work goes on. Anything that depends on it waits too. Only an FRD or an EPIC can be parked. `park.cjs` writes the mark in its status line. | • `park.cjs`<br>• the status line<br>• status.cjs<br>• the routes |
| **acceptance** | Your "yes" at a gate. It runs `accept.cjs`. That saves a numbered copy of the document in the Version log, saves a copy of the Question log, rebuilds the Traceability map, and brings every open change's progress up to date. It also sets the document's status line. | • `accept.cjs`<br>• every gate |

### Kinds of document, and how facts move

| Term | What it means | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| **living document** | A document kept up to date by its own skill. Each change makes a new version, saved when you accept it. | • BRD<br>• PRD<br>• Glossary<br>• the things file<br>• each data model file<br>• the data model's decisions file<br>• Feature catalog<br>• FRD<br>• Stories<br>• Question log |
| **record** | A document that is only ever added to. A later change adds an entry, and nothing already written is changed. | • Discovery brief<br>• EPIC grouping<br>• Change impact report<br>• Version log |
| **generated file** | A file a program rebuilds from other documents. Never edited by hand. | • EPIC sequencing<br>• EPIC mapping<br>• Traceability map |
| **external** | Something you bring in from outside the workflow. | • your requirement files<br>• design files<br>• files brought with a change |
| **each fact is written once** | Each fact is written in one living document only. Every other document refers to it by its ID, such as NFR-2. When the fact changes, only that document changes. When a document does copy text, a program checks the copy matches. | • every document<br>• conventions.md, section 3 |
| **the latest document wins** | A decision at a later gate may change an answer that an earlier document gives. The later document then says the new answer, with a note that names the decision. The earlier document stays as it is, and no change is raised. Later steps copy the later document. | • an FRD's gate<br>• conventions.md, section 3 |
| **the ways back** | Most steps only go forward. When a step finds something an earlier document must settle, it goes back one of a fixed set of ways: a data model decision, a Glossary change, a "?" row in the Feature catalog, a note in the FRD, or a change. A later gate never sends an answer back. | README.md, section 3, "The ways back" |
| **a mistake found later** | When a step finds a mistake in a document you already accepted, it pauses. You raise the mistake as a change. Nothing you accepted is edited outside a change. | • any step<br>• conventions.md, section 17 |
| **the reader's copy** | A copy of a document without its citations, for a reader outside the kit, such as a developer reading a story as a GitHub issue. `reader-copy.cjs` prints it. The document itself never changes. | • `reader-copy.cjs`<br>• `stories-workflow`, when a ready story goes into a tracker |
| **template** | The blank shape of a document. Each document is written from its template. The template's Placeholders part says what each section holds, and is never copied into a document. Text in {curly braces} is a placeholder, and none is left at the gate. | • the templates folder<br>• the examples folder |

### What a document may say

| Term | What it means | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| **the labels for unknowns** | A gap is never filled with a guess. It gets one label: [Not specified in provided sources], Proposed, needs confirmation, TBD, or [Needs source excerpt]. Each gap also names its question, such as "TBD, question 12". | • every document<br>• conventions.md, section 9 |
| **confidence tags** | Every KPI, NFR and measurable claim gets one tag. CONFIRMED: every detail is in a source. TENTATIVE: it is in a source, but incomplete. AI-RECOMMENDED: it is in no source, and you decide at the gate. | • BRD<br>• PRD |
| **cite every specific** | Every number, limit, rule or other specific says where it came from, such as "per claims-requirements-v2.docx" or "per your decision at a gate". Nothing is invented: a specific no source states is TBD or Proposed. | • every document<br>• conventions.md, section 11<br>• conventions.md, section 12 |
| **beyond the client's words** | Every place where a document goes beyond, or differs from, what the client wrote is marked, so a program can list it for you. For example, a Proposed value, an AI-RECOMMENDED item you accepted, or an FRD rule whose source is proposed or decided. | • every gate, before you accept<br>• conventions.md, section 24 |
| **the list for the client** | What the check gives you after you accept, to take back to the client. It has two halves: what we changed or added, for the client to confirm, and what we still need from the client, each worded as a question. | the close of every gate |
| **business language** | Documents say what the user sees and does, never how it is built. Words such as API, database, table or cache never appear, unless a source uses them. | • BRD<br>• PRD<br>• Feature catalog<br>• EPICs<br>• FRDs<br>• stories |
| **a name is not a source** | A title, a feature name or a file name only points at sourced text. A word that appears only in a name never becomes content. | • every document<br>• conventions.md, section 14 |
| **NFRs** | The non-functional requirements. Each one is kept only in the PRD, with an ID such as NFR-2. Every other document lists only the IDs that apply to it. | • PRD<br>• EPIC brief |

### Simple writing

| Term | What it means | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| **simple writing** | The rules every document and every gate message follows, so a reader whose first language isn't English understands each sentence the first time. ### The data model

| Term | What it means | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| **the data model** | What the product keeps information about, written from the Glossary, the BRD and the PRD. For each thing, it gives what people do with it, the rules for each field, and how it moves. It never names or defines a term again, and never comments on the requirements. | • `data-model-workflow`<br>• data-model-design.md<br>• glossary/data-models/ |
| **thing** | Something the product keeps information about, such as a Book, a Loan or a Member. A Glossary word becomes a thing when it has nothing above it, or has details of its own. | • the things file<br>• each data model file |
| **field and choice** | A field is one piece of information a thing holds, like a box on its form, such as a book's title. A choice is one allowed value of a field, such as Paperback for a book's format. | each data model file |
| **action** | Something a person, or the product, does with a thing, such as Borrow or Extend. Each action has the step it happens in, who does it, and its rules. Every thing has an action that makes it, and rows for finding, opening, changing and removing it. | • each data model file<br>• Feature catalog, section 5 |
| **rules table** | One row per field of a thing, with the same six columns every time: Type, Required, Limits, Can two share it, Can change, Starts as. Each rule always becomes the same kind of test. | • each data model file<br>• the FRD's Data section |
| **how it moves** | For a status or a step: a one-line picture of the path, a Moves table with From, To, When and Who, and what each state means, in positive words. A move not in the table is refused. | • each data model file<br>• Feature catalog, section 5 |
| **Adds to {Thing}** | A part of a data model file that adds a field, an action or a move to a thing from an earlier family. Each family's file covers only its own part of the PRD. | each data model file |
| **the things file** | The data model's first file. It lists every thing the product keeps information about, by Glossary family, with its definition copied word for word. Each family names its data model file once. It also lists the words left out, and the product-wide decisions. | • `things-author`<br>• the data model's gate 1 |
| **data model file** | One file per Glossary family, named after the family, such as lending.md. It holds each thing's actions, rules table and moves, and anything the family adds to earlier things. | • `data-model-author`<br>• the data model's gate 2<br>• `feature-author`<br>• `frd-author` |
| **the data model's decisions file** | The data model's own record of decisions, one per product. It holds every data model decision, open or settled, and every Glossary change the data model needs, with its status. | • `things-author`<br>• `data-model-author`<br>• `data-model-workflow`<br>• status.cjs |
| **data model decision (DM-n)** | A gap in the data model that you settle at its gate, numbered DM-1 onward across the product. It is only ever about a rule: a limit, who does an action, or which of two requirements that disagree the data model follows. It never goes into the Question log, and never onto the list for the client. | • the data model's gates<br>• the data model's decisions file<br>• an FRD note "on the basis of DM-n" |
| **product-wide decision** | A data model decision that would be asked the same way in every family's file, such as which roles can act in the first release. `things-author` asks it once, at gate 1, and its answer fills every family's file. | • the things file<br>• the data model's gate 1 |
| **Glossary change (GC-n)** | A word the data model needs that the Glossary doesn't settle, numbered GC-1 onward. The data model collects them all. Once every data model file is accepted, they go together to `glossary-disposition`, in one run. Then the data model is brought in step. Each one is marked as it goes: "To raise", then "Raised", then "Settled in the Glossary on {date}" or "Left open on {date}", then "Brought into the data model on {date}". | • the data model's decisions file<br>• `data-model-workflow`, steps 3 and 4<br>• `glossary-disposition` |
| **"? (DM-n)" and "? (GC-n)"** | How a data model file shows a gap: a question mark, then the decision or Glossary change it waits on. Each thing lists its open ones under "Decisions to make" and "Waiting on the Glossary", with the recommended answer. | • each data model file<br>• the things file |
| **where each gap goes** | The table that sends each data model gap to exactly one document, by its kind. Most become a data model decision. A word the Glossary doesn't settle becomes a Glossary change. A question the PRD left open takes the Question log's recommended answer, marked Proposed. A new action big enough to be a feature is written only when the Glossary names it. | data-model-design.md, section 2 |
| **asked again first** | A data model decision answered "I don't know yet", and a Glossary change left open, are never forgotten. `status.cjs` lists them under "Data model, still open". The skill that owns each one asks it again first, at its next run. They hold up no other step. | • status.cjs<br>• `things-author`<br>• `data-model-author` |
| **"Is it big enough to be a feature?"** | The one test the data model and `feature-author` both use. Making, finding, opening, changing and removing a thing are small enough to be a rule. Any other action, such as one that moves a thing to another state, is big enough to be a feature. A field is always small enough to be a rule. | • data-model-design.md, section 2<br>• Feature catalog, section 5 |

## 9. Handling a change: being rebuilt

The change workflow is being redesigned. These are its pieces as they are today, so you can find them. They will be described in full once the new design is in the kit.

**The ideas, as the kit has them today**

| Term | What it means today |
|---|---|
| **a change** | One thing you ask to change after documents exist. It can come from the client, from you, from a step that found a mistake, from a late answer, or from the Feature catalog's gate. Each change gets a number, 001 onward, and its own folder. |
| **a late answer** | An answer to an open question that arrives after the documents that use it exist. It comes in as a change. |
| **an ask** | One thing a change asks for. A statement that asks for several things is split, one ask per row. |
| **kind of change** | One of: Net new, Extend or enhance, Update or optimize, Already covered, or Blocked. The kind sets the path. |
| **Feature Fit** | The method that says whether a change's words exist in the Glossary. Its verdict is Fits, Extend, New capability or Blocked. It comes before the update test. |
| **the update test** | Fixed steps that decide whether a change alters an existing FRD rule, value, limit or wording. Each rule compared gets a result: same, opposite, changed value or new. A changed rule makes the kind Update or optimize. A reworded requirement with no rule touched is "wording only". |
| **linked item and judgement** | A linked item is anything linked to where the change lands, found through the Traceability map or a drafted document. A judgement is `feature-impact`'s call on one linked item: No change, Update, New story or Split. A data model row gets No change or Update only. |
| **a piece in progress** | Whatever is drafted but not yet accepted: the things file or a data model file, the Feature catalog, the EPIC grouping, an FRD, or an EPIC from when its Story list exists until every story is ready. |
| **size of a change** | Small: the change is folded into the piece in progress. Large: a feature moves into or out of the piece in progress, or regroups two or more pieces, or an EPIC gets more than one new or split story. Not sized: nothing is in progress. |
| **parked by a large change** | When a change is large and you choose to run the path, `park.cjs` parks every FRD and EPIC in progress until the change is applied. Its status line reads "parked on {date}, change {nnn}". |
| **the path and the recommendation** | The recommendation names the workflows to run again, from which steps, for which items. Your choices at the change's gate: Run the path, Insert it, Copy the wording, Re-run the whole workflow, Close it, or Correct the analysis. |
| **starting again from nothing** | A re-run of the whole workflow, from `requirements-workflow`, step 1. It is offered when a change reaches more than half of the business requirements, and happens only when you ask for it in words. Everything in the project folder except sources moves to an archive folder, by hand. |
| **a change's progress and its closing** | Each accepted change keeps a progress file, one row for each document it sends work to. `track-change.cjs` updates it at every acceptance. When the last row is accepted, `close-change.cjs` writes a closing entry, and the change closes by itself. |
